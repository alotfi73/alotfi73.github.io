Finance and method
Assumptions this tool makes
  • Correlations are carbon steel at their stated design conditions; alloy factors are applied, pressure corrections are not.
  • Capital from 1998 correlations is at best AACE Class 4 (−15 to −30 % low, +20 to +50 % high) and nearer Class 5 once 27 years of escalation are counted.
  • nth-plant economics: no first-of-a-kind premium. For a first commercial unit the result is a floor.
  • Byproducts are credited at the price entered. Government credits are per unit produced; eligibility rules are yours to check.

    Method and sources

    The calculation follows the U.S. national-lab conventions, in order. Equipment is priced from power-law correlations, C = Cb(S/Sb)n, escalated by the Chemical Engineering Plant Cost Index and multiplied by a material factor and a location factor. Purchased cost becomes installed cost with the DOE/NETL distributive factors, a Lang factor or your own factor, and installed cost climbs the NETL ladder: bare erected cost, EPC cost, total plant cost, total overnight cost and total as-spent capital.

    The headline number is the price that makes the after-tax net present value zero at your discount rate, the way NREL reports a minimum selling price. The fixed-charge-rate, capital-recovery and straight-line methods run on the same inputs beside it, because the spread between them is the quickest check that the financing assumptions are doing what you think.

    Three things are easy to get wrong and are handled here: land is in the overnight cost but is never depreciated; a nominal discount rate is only paired with inflating costs; and debt drawn during construction accrues interest until start-up. The lesson explains each of them.
