About this site

Chemical Process Engineering shares knowledge, tools and practice examples for chemical engineering students and engineers.

What the site is for

Chemical process engineering is learned by working problems. This site puts the explanation, the calculation and the standard on the same page.

  • Lessons explain process equipment and methods, from heat exchangers and compressors to separators and distillation, with worked examples and the codes and standards that apply.
  • Calculators and tools run in your browser and show their working, so you can check every number by hand.
  • Practice examples come with full solutions, for students preparing for exams and engineers checking a design or a vendor's numbers.

Every page names its assumptions, marks illustrative numbers as illustrative and lists its sources with the date they were checked.

Educational content. The pages explain methods and show typical values. Codes, standards and your company's specifications govern any real design.

Who runs it

The site is owned and edited by Alireza Lotfollahzade, EIT, in Metro Vancouver. He holds an M.Sc. in chemical engineering and is registered as an Engineer-in-Training with Engineers and Geoscientists BC.

His background spans chemical engineering research and industrial process engineering. His work centres on steady-state and dynamic process simulation in Aspen Plus, Aspen HYSYS, Aspen Adsorption and Aspen Custom Modeler; on techno-economic analysis; and on adsorption systems (TSA and PSA) for CO₂ capture and hydrogen production.

He studied and carried out research at Sharif University of Technology, Concordia University and the University of Alberta, where he worked on methane pyrolysis for low-carbon hydrogen. His research has been published in Energy & Environmental Science and Chemical Engineering Journal.

Contact and corrections

Found an error in the content, or something on the site that isn’t working? Email support@chemprocesseng.com.

It helps to include:

  • For a content error: the page and section, what it says, and what you think it should say, with a source if you have one.
  • For a calculator or tool: the inputs you used, the result you got and the result you expected.
  • For a page that doesn’t work: the address, what happened, and your browser and device.

If you have a GitHub account, you can also open an issue on GitHub.
